How to Find Direct Shippers for Your Trucking Company

October 30, 2026 · Aras, dytch · 7 min read

Direct shippers are companies that own the freight: manufacturers, distributors, retailers, farms. They hire carriers without a broker in the middle. Carriers who work direct routinely report 20–40% better rates per mile than load-board freight, on loads that repeat. The question is how a carrier with five or fifty trucks gets in front of them. This is the playbook.

1. Niche down before you reach out

“We haul anything, anywhere” is not a pitch. It is what 91.5% of US carriers — everyone running ten trucks or fewer, per the American Trucking Associations’ 2025 data — can say. Shippers hire specialists. A steel fabricator wants the flatbed carrier who knows tarping and overweight permits. A food distributor wants the reefer carrier who logs temperatures. Pick one freight type, one industry, one geography. You can expand later; you start specific.

2. Build the list from the ground up

Open a map of your lanes and write down every industrial park, distribution center and manufacturer along them. Cross-reference with NAICS codes (311 is food manufacturing, 332 is fabricated metal) and public records. You are looking for mid-sized shippers without a dedicated transportation department, where the operations manager still answers the phone. That list is the asset everything else runs on.

3. Reach the person who books the freight

On LinkedIn, search “logistics manager”, “transportation manager” or “shipping coordinator” filtered by your industry and region. By email, write to that person about their lanes, not your services. One specific line about their company, one line about your equipment and authority, one ask. Our companion piece on the math explains why this beats another hour on the boards.

4. Get on the bid list before it is written

Contract freight is awarded in annual bids, usually run in the first or last quarter. Shipper guides (Worldwide Express’s RFP guide is the standard reference) tell buyers to invite three to six qualified providers, and the whole process runs 8–12 weeks. The carrier’s window is the first two or three weeks, while the shipper is still preparing data. If they first hear your name after the invitations go out, you are quoting against the board for another year.

5. Be the backup carrier first

Nobody fires their primary carrier for a stranger. They will, however, give a prepared carrier one load when the regular guy drops it at 4pm on a Friday. Have your MC number, insurance and lane sheet ready to send in one email. Nail the audition load, then ask for more after two or three clean runs. Most direct accounts grow from exactly this start.
