What a CDL Driver Lead Costs in 2026

October 30, 2026 · Aras, dytch · 6 min read

Ask three vendors what a driver lead costs and you will get three prices for three different products. The word covers shared list records, exclusive leads, and generated applications, and they are not interchangeable. Here is what each one is, what it costs, and where the money actually goes.

The three things sold as a “driver lead”

ModelWhat you getTypical priceThe catch
Shared listDriver records sold to several carriers at once$2–6 per recordThe driver hears from four recruiters the same afternoon
Exclusive leadA driver who raised a hand, sold to one carrierSeveral times the shared priceExclusive to you, but the driver applied elsewhere too
Generated applicationsYour own ads, your own form, applications that belong to youMedia spend plus a feeYou own the pipeline, but you carry the media risk

Boostpoint’s 2026 Social Job Advertising Benchmark, covering 891 managed campaigns, puts the median cost of a generated CDL driver application at $26.86, with the middle half of campaigns between $17.00 and $42.31. Shared, phone-verified records sell for as little as $1.99–$6 (dry van company driver), $5–$9 (flatbed) and $10–$20 (owner-operator) through vendors like Route66Leads. Managed recruiting packages bundling ads and creative run $650–$1,490 a month plus daily ad spend (Transportation Code’s published tiers).

The number that matters is not the lead price

Cost per seat is cost per lead divided by lead-to-hire rate. A $4 shared record that never answers costs more per hire than a $27 application from a driver who applied to your job four minutes ago. Vendors themselves admit 20–40% of unverified leads never respond. Before any vendor call, do the arithmetic with your own contact and hire rates, not theirs.

Why driver campaigns cost what they cost

The auction is not the problem; the form is. Boostpoint’s data shows CDL campaigns convert about 8% of ad clicks into finished applications, against 20% for warehouse roles, and that the conversion rate explains 70% of the cost variation. Campaigns converting 5–10% pay a median $23.13 per applicant; campaigns at 10–20% pay $11.11. The levers are boring and effective: fewer questions, a form that opens inside the app, and no resume upload a driver does not have on his phone.

What we would tell any small fleet

Buy shared records only if your recruiters dial fast and dial first. Buy generated applications if you want a pipeline you own. Either way, judge the vendor on cost per responsive applicant, in writing, before the first invoice. And put your lanes, pay and home time in front of drivers who are not job hunting — that is where the cheap, good applicants live.
